# Stage transitions and cinematic set pieces in anime-style 3D fighting and duel action games

Scope note for the report writer: web access was limited. Several primary pages (TV Tropes, Gematsu, the Unreal Engine developer interview, Siliconera) returned HTTP 403, and many results were search-engine summaries, not full pages. Anything under "Cited Findings" has a source. Anything labelled **[Observation]** under "Inferences" is the researcher's own memory of playing or watching the games. It was not confirmed by a source in this session, so treat it as plausible but unverified. The last item in each "Inferences" list applies the findings to the 65-second, four-location sword-duel trailer.

## 1. Demon Slayer: The Hinokami Chronicles 1 and 2 (CyberConnect2): story boss battles, QTE set pieces, phase changes, Ultimate Arts, camera

### Takeaway
Hinokami boss fights are built in phases. Normal 3D arena combat alternates with cutscene breaks, and the fight ends in a scripted cinematic quick-time-event (QTE) sequence that delivers the final blow. Reviewers consistently call this the best part of both games. The location changes happen in the cutscene or QTE breaks between phases, not through traversal during play.

### Cited Findings
- Boss battles in Hinokami 1 "have Quick Time Events (QTE) at the end of them". The reviewer calls this a return from CyberConnect2's other games and says the bosses "included their own mechanics that change up the usual normal pace of combat." — [Steam Deck HQ review](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/demon-slayer-the-hinokami-chronicles/)
- "At the conclusion to a boss fight a cinematic Quick Time Event sequence plays out to deliver the final blow." Reviewers praise the "bombastic boss fights and some stunning cutscenes" as what sells an otherwise short story mode. — reviews surfaced via search: [Press Start](https://press-start.com.au/reviews/ps5-reviews/2021/10/14/demon-slayer-the-hinokami-chronicles-review-a-remarkable-retelling/), [Cubed3](https://www.cubed3.com/games/reviews/playstation-5/demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-the-hinokami-chronicles), [Siliconera](https://vip-develop.siliconera.com/review-demon-slayer-the-hinokami-chronicles-game-feels-like-a-first-step). Search summaries only; the exact quote's attribution between these three was not confirmed.
- A counterpoint: one review says "the majority of the story mode is taken up by slowly wandering around rather bland environments before taking on a battle." — same review set as above (summary)
- Akaza (Mugen Train arc, fought as Rengoku) effectively has two health bars. He regenerates fully the first time the bar is emptied. His second phase adds a "Rage Mode", after which he is exhausted and open to attack. — [TV Tropes (via search summary)](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/DemonSlayerKimetsuNoYaibaTheHinokamiChronicles); [Steam guide: Rengoku vs Akaza](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2629901680)
- Akaza and Rui were later made playable as DLC, "the first playable antagonists in the game." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demon_Slayer:_Kimetsu_no_Yaiba_%E2%80%93_The_Hinokami_Chronicles)
- Hinokami 1's Adventure mode lets you "relive memorable moments from the Demon Slayer: Kimetsu no Yaiba anime through the eyes of its protagonist, Tanjiro Kamado." It covers the story up to the Mugen Train arc. — [Nintendo announcement](https://nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/become-a-part-of-the-story-in-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-the-hinokami-chronicles)
- Hinokami 2 (released 5 August 2025) adapts the Entertainment District, Swordsmith Village and Hashira Training arcs in story mode. The pre-launch trailer featured Tengen Uzui, Daki and Gyutaro. — [Bleeding Cool](https://bleedingcool.com/games/demon-slayer-hinokami-chronicles-2-drops-pre-launch-trailer/); [TheGamer preview](https://www.thegamer.com/demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-the-hinokami-chronicles-2-preview-summer-game-fest/)
- Noisy Pixel's Hinokami 2 preview describes the first encounter with Daki in the Entertainment District as a "nicely directed clash" that blends the studio's "trademark cinematic flair" with faster, more responsive controls. Later encounters "ramped up quickly". — [Noisy Pixel](https://noisypixel.net/demon-slayer-hinokami-chronicles-2-preview/)
- IGN's Hinokami 2 review calls the boss fights "flashy and engaging throughout" and "easily the highlight of the story mode." — [IGN via search summary; Game8 review index](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-the-hinokami-chronicles-2-review). The IGN URL itself was not retrieved.
- One Hinokami 2 review says the story-mode cutscenes stay worth watching "despite their condensed nature." It singles out the Tengen vs. Gyutaro battle for its music. — [One More Game](https://onemoregame.ph/2025/08/demon-slayer-hinokami-chronicles-2-review/)
- Hinokami 2 is described as a "great recap before Infinity Castle". The Infinity Castle arc is not in the game. — [Final Weapon review](https://finalweapon.net/2025/07/31/demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-the-hinokami-chronicles-2-review-impressions/)
- CyberConnect2's general camera rule from the Naruto Storm era also applies here: Matsuyama said "the camera moves according to the real-time animation so that the character is always in the picture." — [GamesRadar (Storm 1 interview)](https://www.gamesradar.com/naruto-ultimate-ninja-storm-2/)

### Inferences
- **[Observation]** Hinokami boss structure: the boss has several health gauges. Emptying one cuts to a short in-engine cutscene, often with a button-prompt QTE. Play then resumes with new boss moves and sometimes a changed setting. The final gauge ends in a longer cinematic QTE sequence that recreates the anime's key cuts, such as Tanjiro's Hinokami Kagura against Rui on Mt. Natagumo.
- **[Observation]** The Mugen Train arc is the clearest location progression in Hinokami 1. Tanjiro fights Enmu on the night-time train roof against wind and motion. After Enmu fuses with the train, the setting becomes the flesh-covered train. After the crash, Rengoku fights Akaza in an open field at dawn. Each location change is carried by a cutscene beat (merge, derailment, Akaza's arrival), never by the characters travelling on screen.
- **[Observation]** Versus mode is a 2v2 tag 3D arena fighter. The camera sits behind the controlled fighter and is locked onto the opponent. Stages are bounded arenas with invisible walls and no mid-match stage transitions. Ultimate Arts use the "hit-confirm then cinematic" pattern: the opening strike lands in gameplay, then the camera takes over for a short letterboxed sequence. Those sequences use ukiyo-e-style water and fire effects that echo ufotable's anime, plus tight close-ups and a final wide shot of the impact.
- Applying this to the trailer: Hinokami moves fights between locations at the phase break. A big hit or a story beat hard-cuts to a new setting, so no travel animation is ever needed. The documented "cinematic QTE at the end" structure means the most polished, anime-faithful shots go at the climax. That suggests saving the trailer's most authored, storyboard-like shots for the infinite castle finale.

### Gaps
- No official CyberConnect2 or SEGA document or interview describing the exact boss-phase or QTE system for Hinokami 1 or 2 was retrieved. TV Tropes and Gematsu pages returned 403.
- No source in this session described specific Hinokami 2 boss set pieces, such as the Gyutaro rooftop phase or Hantengu and Gyokko in Swordsmith Village, or whether Hinokami 2 adds dual or combo Ultimate Arts. The researcher believes it does, but this is unconfirmed.
- No CEDEC talk on Hinokami camera work was found.

## 2. Naruto Shippuden: Ultimate Ninja Storm series (CyberConnect2): giant boss set pieces, terrain destruction, moving between areas, Secret Techniques, chase and leap animation

### Takeaway
The Storm series built its reputation on "epic boss battles" that mix QTEs with direct fighting against very large opponents. CyberConnect2 temporarily removed them in *Storm Generations* to make it play "more like a fighting game", which shows they are a deliberate cinematic layer and not part of the core fighter. Location shifts and destruction happen at phase breaks, carried by cinematics.

### Cited Findings
- Storm 2 boss battles "returned, with quick time events and cutscenes." — [Wikipedia: Storm 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto_Shippuden:_Ultimate_Ninja_Storm_2)
- Matsuyama said Storm 3 would bring back very large bosses and combine QTEs with direct fighting against them. — [Gematsu interview, 2012 (search summary)](https://www.gematsu.com/2012/10/interview-cyberconnect2-ceo-hiroshi-matsuyama-on-narutimate-storm-3-hack-and-jojos-bizarre-adventure)
- *Storm Generations* dropped the epic bosses. Matsuyama said they wanted it to play more like a fighting game. — same Gematsu interview (summary); see also [Siliconera on Generations](https://www.siliconera.com/cyberconnect2s-kage-on-making-naruto-shippuden-ultimate-ninja-storm-generations/)
- Storm 3 features "boss battles, including quick time events requiring strategy to defeat the opponent". Critics praised its cinematic boss battles. Matsuyama promised the return of "epic boss battles." "Several arenas are interactive, making a player lose a fight if they leave an area" (a ring-out option). The *Full Burst* re-release adds "enhanced cinematics." — [Wikipedia: Storm 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto_Shippuden:_Ultimate_Ninja_Storm_3)
- For Storm 4, Matsuyama said boss battles would return in story mode as the story pushes up to the series climax. — [Gematsu 2015 (search summary)](https://www.gematsu.com/2015/04/cyberconnect2-boss-talks-new-naruto-shippuden-ultimate-ninja-storm-4-features)
- Storm 4 set pieces cited by fans: Naruto and Sasuke's combination attack sends Madara's own "Colony Drop" meteor back at him. In another, Madara combines his Susano'o with Limbo clones while Sasuke, in his own Susano'o, chases them "in a raging storm", before Madara adds "Planetary Devastation". — [TV Tropes: Awesome, Storm 4 (search summary)](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Awesome/NarutoShippudenUltimateNinjaStorm4)
- Players criticised the Madara fight because he keeps retreating ("runs away from you rather than giving me a proper battle"). — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/349040/discussions/0/451850849200974672)
- CyberConnect2's camera follows the real-time animation so the character always stays in frame. — [GamesRadar (Storm 1 interview)](https://www.gamesradar.com/naruto-ultimate-ninja-storm-2/)

### Inferences
- **[Observation]** The canonical "fight that travels" is Storm 2's Naruto vs. Pain. The fight starts in the flattened crater of Konoha after Shinra Tensei. Each phase break is a cinematic plus QTE that shifts the scale: Pain's Deva Path pulls Naruto in, Naruto enters Sage Mode, the Nine-Tails transformation follows, and the fight ends with Chibaku Tensei, a gravitational sphere of terrain rising into the sky. The setting changes because the technique reshapes the land, not because the characters fly somewhere new.
- **[Observation]** Storm 4's final Naruto vs. Sasuke fight at the Valley of the End escalates in scale. It starts as a ground duel on the river, moves to Kurama vs. Susano'o at kaiju scale (the Hashirama and Madara statues are destroyed), then shrinks back to a ruined, exhausted fistfight. It ends with the Rasengan vs. Chidori clash as a button-mash QTE. The same location reads as three different settings because destruction changes it between phases. That idea fits the burning-rooftops beat well.
- **[Observation]** Secret Techniques (Ultimate Jutsu) use a hit-confirm: an opening move must connect in gameplay, then a cinematic plays, often in a separate abstract or stylised space, such as sky backdrops or the inside of a technique. Play returns to the arena afterwards. This lets the fight briefly go somewhere else without any traversal.
- **[Observation]** Movement language that avoids floatiness in Storm:
  - The ninja run: body pitched forward with arms trailing, low to the ground, kicking up dust.
  - Chakra Dash: a near-linear burst with a chakra-coloured trail and a dust plume, ending in a skid.
  - Tree-hopping: short, flat arcs from branch to branch, each landing a crouched contact pose that pushes off at once, not one long parabola.
  - Water-walking: water stages show ripple rings at every footfall and spray on dashes.
  - Wall-running (Storm 4): the camera tilts so the wall reads as the ground plane.
- Applying this to the trailer: the Naruto pattern is "travel along surfaces, not through the air". Short contact-heavy hops, wall-runs and dashes with ground contact read as speed. Long jumps read as floating. For the temple-to-street move, a Secret-Technique-style cutaway into a stylised space works as a transition: the hit lands in the courtyard, the cinematic plays in an abstract ink or rain space, and the camera returns on the rainy street.

### Gaps
- No CEDEC or GDC talk by CyberConnect2 on Storm boss-battle direction or camera was retrieved, and no developer description of specific set pieces such as Pain, Kurama or Madara. The Gematsu pages were blocked (403). Only summaries were available.
- No source confirmed the exact Storm 4 wall-running mechanics or specific terrain-destruction systems; Wikipedia only mentions ring-outs.

## 3. Jujutsu Kaisen: Cursed Clash, One Piece (Pirate Warriors and Burning Blood), Dragon Ball FighterZ and Sparking! Zero: knock-through transitions, Dramatic Finishes, destruction, rush chases, flight that reads as speed

### Takeaway
The clearest documented example of "knock the opponent through the scenery into a new area" is in Arc System Works games: DBFZ's Destructive Finish and stage-destruction transitions, and Strive's Wall Break (Q5). In 3D arena fighters (Sparking! Zero, Cursed Clash), destruction mostly damages the arena locally or changes the whole field, as with Domain Expansion, instead of moving the fight to a different place.

### Cited Findings
- **DBFZ Dramatic Finishes** depend on the character pairing and the stage. Some need the stage destroyed first, usually by a Level 3 super. Some check the stage's time of day. The finishing hit must be a neutral standing Heavy or a Vanish attack. Examples: Goku vs. Frieza on a destroyed Planet Namek, and Teen Gohan vs. Cell at the Cell Games Arena (stage destroyed, Goku on neither team). — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103042/dragon-ball-fighterz---how-to-do-dramatic-finishes-and-dramatic-intros); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/dragon-ball-fighterz-how-perform-dramatic-finish); [Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/dragon-ball-fighterz-how-to-activate-all-dramatic-finishers/)
- **DBFZ Destructive Finish vs. stage destruction:** players describe a separate mechanic where a KO by certain Level 3 finishers changes the location to its destroyed version, and report that the transition is inconsistent. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/678950/discussions/0/1741103267282800175/) (community source)
- **Sparking! Zero** is officially sold on "massive and destructible fighting arenas". The reveal trailer shows a rock formation destroyed when Goku is launched into it. Dragon Dash returns for high-speed movement. A new Ki action, "Vanishing Assault", lets you "approach an opponent instantly and pounce." — [Bandai Namco announcement](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/pt_br/news/dragon-ball-sparking-zero-announcement); [GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/dragon-ball-sparking-zero-trailer-reveals-how-its-a-genuine-budokai-tenkaichi-sequel/1100-6521990/); [Gematsu, Power vs. Speed trailer](https://gematsu.com/2024/03/dragon-ball-sparking-zero-power-vs-speed-trailer-and-gameplay-showcase-new-systems-and-11-new-characters-announced) (summary; the page itself was 403)
- Sparking! Zero players say it lacks FighterZ-style destructive finishes. The only large stage-changing destruction they cite is an Ultimate that misses on Namek, which "works like a stage transition". — [GameFAQs board](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/439864-dragon-ball-sparking-zero/80648648) (community source; may predate patches)
- **Jujutsu Kaisen: Cursed Clash** (a 2v2 arena fighter): the official page lists "gimmicks that cause the stage to collapse when attacked", and Domain Expansions "evolve the entire field", trapping players inside the Domain. — [Bandai Namco product page](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/games/jujutsu-kaisen-cursed-clash)
- Cursed Clash reviewed poorly: Gaming Trend gave it 25/100, and Push Square called it "unfortunately cursed trash". Final Weapon's headline speaks of "fun gameplay buried within lackluster presentation". In short, it is a weak example of execution even though the concepts are relevant. — [Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/jujutsu-kaisen-cursed-clash-review-absolutely-cursed/); [Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/jujutsu-kaisen-cursed-clash); [Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2024/02/09/jujutsu-kaisen-cursed-clash-review-fun-gameplay-buried-within-lackluster-presentation)
- Arc System Works brought its limited-animation 3D method from Guilty Gear Xrd to DBFZ; a talk/video titled "The Animation of Guilty Gear Xrd & Dragon Ball FighterZ" exists. — [Amara transcript listing](https://amara.org/videos/bDmpkclvfB5I/en/3103527)

### Inferences
- **[Observation]** DBFZ's stage-destruction transition: a Level 3 super finishes the last character in the corner, the camera follows the victim blasted horizontally across the landscape, and the next round loads on the scorched or cratered version of the same stage. The camera follows the projectile body, so the travel reads as violent displacement, not voluntary flight.
- **[Observation]** How Budokai Tenkaichi and Sparking! Zero make flight read as speed:
  - Dragon Dash is a near-horizontal line with the body pitched about 45 degrees forward, an aura trail and radial speed lines. The camera trails close behind, so the background streaks.
  - Vanish/teleport moves remove the in-between frames entirely: a blink and afterimage, then the attacker reappears behind the target.
  - Rushes start or end with a shockwave ring and dust on contact.
  - Ultimates that hit the ground leave craters and lingering smoke.
  - No arcs: speed is rendered as straight vectors plus instant arrival.
- **[Observation]** One Piece Burning Blood (Spike Chunsoft) uses 3D arenas, such as Marineford, where characters are smashed into walls and debris. One Piece Pirate Warriors (Omega Force) uses large musou maps where location changes are handled by the mission structure and cutscenes, not inside fights. Pirate Warriors is therefore a weak reference for duel transitions. This was not verified against reviews in this session.
- **[Observation]** Cursed Clash's Domain Expansion shows a "location change without moving": the field is replaced around both fighters, as with Gojo's Unlimited Void or Sukuna's Malevolent Shrine. This maps directly to the trailer's surreal infinite castle. The castle can arrive around the fighters instead of them travelling into it.
- Applying this to the trailer, three documented transition types are available:
  1. Knock-through: the victim is driven through scenery and the camera follows the body into the new area (DBFZ, Strive).
  2. Field replacement: the world is swapped around the fighters (Domain Expansion).
  3. Instant relocation: a vanish or teleport with afterimage cuts out travel frames entirely (Sparking! Zero).

  None of these involves a visible long jump.

### Gaps
- No direct sources were found for One Piece Burning Blood or Pirate Warriors set pieces or stage destruction (searches did not return them).
- No official Bandai Namco or Spike Chunsoft description of Sparking! Zero's camera, sonic booms or afterimage effects was retrieved.
- No source found on whether Cursed Clash's stage collapse moves fighters to a new sub-area.

## 4. Sekiro and Ghost of Tsushima: grounded duels, duel camera, weight, scenic framing

### Takeaway
Ghost of Tsushima's duels and standoffs are documented as deliberate samurai-cinema homage. Nate Fox cites Sanjuro's climactic duel as the source of the standoff system. Wind, tens of thousands of leaf particles and a chosen third-person framing carry the atmosphere. In both games weight comes from fighters who stay on the ground, and from the clash and deflect itself, not from movement through the air.

### Cited Findings
- Fox has said Sanjuro's climactic duel directly informed the standoff system. Art director Jason Connell said the game had "upwards of 60,000 individual leaf particles" blowing in the wind, some waiting "to be kicked up by a warrior in a duel." — [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/creating-the-way-of-the-warrior-for-ghost-of-tsushima/); Sanjuro link via search summary of Kurosawa coverage ([GamesRadar Kurosawa Mode](https://gamesradar.com/ghost-of-tsushima-kurosawa-mode-explained-how-it-changes-the-game-and-why))
- Fox said a third-person camera is needed for "staring down an enemy in a duel". VFX Voice also notes that "the wind makes for aesthetically dramatic moments but also serves as a navigation device for players." — [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/creating-the-way-of-the-warrior-for-ghost-of-tsushima/)
- Connell: "Everything moves—clothes, hair, trees, bushes, grass." He also said, "So many Kurosawa movies open with really wide expansive shots, where a person is like drowning in a sea of green." He shot about 15,000 reference photos on Tsushima and "looked for good compositions and interesting colour choices" in samurai films. — [Flicks.com.au](https://www.flicks.com.au/features/how-the-hit-ps4-game-ghost-of-tsushima-was-influenced-by-kurosawa-and-samurai-cinema/)
- Fox: the team wanted to "draw upon samurai cinema" for a tense yet "serene" atmosphere before a duel. Duels ask players to adapt to the opponent without other weapons as "crutches". — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/ghost-tsushima-duels/)
- Locations were designed to be "the perfect photographer's dream". The landscape and minimalist style drew on Shadow of the Colossus. — [Wikipedia: Ghost of Tsushima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_of_Tsushima)
- Reviewers describe the standoff camera: it moves behind Jin's shoulder as an enemy approaches for a one-on-one, and the camera "cut to show the two combatants approaching one another, then zoomed in with a cinematic letterboxing effect." — search summaries of [Smashpad review](https://smashpad.com/ghost-of-tsushima-review/) and [GamesRadar trailer breakdown](https://www.gamesradar.com/ghost-of-tsushima-gameplay-trailer-breakdown/)
- Kurosawa Mode adds black and white, film grain, a changed sound mix and "more dramatic wind". It was first called "Samurai Cinema" and renamed with the Kurosawa estate's blessing. A critic notes that cutscenes in the mode can become "simple shot-reverse-shot affairs". — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/ghost-of-tsushima-kurosawa-mode-explained-how-it-changes-the-game-and-why); [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/ghost-of-tsushima-kurosawa-mode)
- Duel locations listed by guides: Ryuzo at Kaneda Castle, then Castle Shimura; Lord Shimura at Omi Lake; Kojiro (the Six Blades mythic tale) in a hidden cave east of Omi Monastery. Fans rank the "Duel of Crashing Waves" (vs. Tomotsugu) and the "Duel Under Autumn Leaves" highest for scenery. — [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/ghost-of-tsushima/duels/z3d6e4); [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/ghost-of-tsushima-six-blades-of-kojiro-mythic-tale-duel-locations/); [DualShockers ranking](https://www.dualshockers.com/ghost-of-tsushima-all-duels-ranked/). The guides disagree on exact positions.
- Sucker Punch's GDC 2021 combat talk, Theodore Fishman's "Honoring the Blade: Lethality and Combat Balance", covers making combat honor the samurai fantasy (lethality). — [YouTube/GDC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dKS2kaI3aXE). Its content was not retrieved.
- **Sekiro:** posture is the core mechanic. A well-timed deflect "deals massive posture damage to the enemy while taking none yourself". Genichiro is the skill check that forces mastery of posture and deflect. — [Push Square guide](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2019/03/guide_sekiro_shadows_die_twice_combat_-_posture_deflections_shinobi_prosthetic_and_stealth); [SuperJump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/)
- Sekiro's lock-on camera is criticised when your back is against a wall. It can block the view of you or the enemy. — [TV Tropes YMMV (summary)](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/YMMV/SekiroShadowsDieTwice)

### Inferences
- **[Observation]** Sekiro frames its key duels as scenic set pieces chosen for their backdrop:
  - Genichiro in the prologue: a moonlit field of silver grass.
  - Genichiro again: the top of Ashina Castle, with wind, rooftop tiles and the valley below.
  - The final Sword Saint Isshin: a vast field of pampas grass by the Ashina Reservoir, with the burning castle beyond.

  The phase changes in the final fight (Genichiro, then Isshin, then Isshin with spear and gun) happen in the same place. The escalation comes from cutscene beats and from the lighting and weather changing, not from moving somewhere else.
- **[Observation]** Weight cues common to Sekiro and Ghost:
  - Characters almost never leave the ground except for short, fast hops.
  - Every deflect gets a hit-stop of a few frames, a large orange spark burst and a sharp metallic "ting".
  - Clashes push both fighters back with skidding feet and dust.
  - Big attacks have long, readable wind-ups with a glint or a red kanji perilous-attack warning, so the release feels heavy.
  - The killing blow (Deathblow in Sekiro, the duel finisher in Ghost) is a held pose, then a blood spray, then the body drops on a delay.
- **[Observation]** Ghost's standoff shot grammar:
  1. A wide establishing shot with both fighters small in the landscape (the "drowning in a sea of green" composition).
  2. Wind and particles move across the frame.
  3. The camera slowly pushes in to over-the-shoulder, with letterbox bars.
  4. Stillness, then a very short burst of violence and a one-cut kill.
- Applying this to the trailer: the mountain temple courtyard is the natural "Ghost/Sekiro" location. Open on a wide, still shot with drifting leaves or petals and a slow push-in, so the stillness makes the first clash feel heavy. Keep this location's fighters entirely on the ground.

### Gaps
- No Sucker Punch GDC talk content specific to the duel or standoff camera was retrieved. The GDC 2021 talk title is known but its content was not.
- No FromSoftware interview on Sekiro's duel camera or arena choice was found.
- No source confirmed that Ghost's named duel arenas were picked by the designers for scenery. The evidence is indirect: Connell's photography and composition comments, plus fan rankings.

## 5. Other references: Guilty Gear Strive wall breaks, Granblue, Kingdom Hearts, Genshin and Honkai Star Rail ultimates, Bayonetta and Devil May Cry, God of War camera

### Takeaway
Guilty Gear Strive's Wall Break is the best-documented "fight moves to a new area" mechanic. Arc System Works says it was designed to be seamless, with no loading cut, and to make players "celebrate breaking the wall." It works with Arc's limited-animation method, documented at GDC 2015, which is the main known recipe for making 3D anime characters look weighty and not floaty.

### Cited Findings
- **Strive Wall Break (official):** continuous attacks at the stage edge break the wall, which transitions the fight to a new area and gives the attacker a "Positive Bonus" (faster Tension gain for a short window). — [Guilty Gear Strive official battle system page](https://www.guiltygear.com/ggst/en/battle/systems/). Patch 1.29 added Burst Gauge refill during Positive Bonus — [MP1st](https://mp1st.com/title-updates-and-patches/guilty-gear-strive-update-1-29-punches-out-this-aug-24-for-johnny-dlc-and-more)
- A wall-broken character first "slam[s] against the wall and slowly slide[s] down". Further hits break it and move play into a new part of the stage. — [Niche Gamer summary of the Famitsu interview with Ishiwatari and Katano](https://nichegamer.com/guilty-gear-strive-directors-insist-i-no-was-not-censored-but-more-accessible-along-with-gameplay/) (secondhand)
- Design intent (director Akira Katano): to break the impression "that the same situation can be repeated multiple times in a row", while adjusting it "so that the player will celebrate breaking the wall rather than feel disappointed." — [Unreal Engine developer interview (search excerpt; page 403)](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/how-guilty-gear--strive--hits-an-ultra-combo-with-groundbreaking-visuals-and-gameplay)
- Genki Mamada (Arc System Works): "we wanted to focus on a seamless design, a transition between stages… we wanted Strive to be seamless—that there's no cut-off." Many other games' stage transitions have "loading screens in the middle." "This design integrated the background and stage transitions with the fighting mechanic." "You can actually see the different production stages if you transition into different areas of the stages." Kitazono cites a stage "where the giant statue of the Buddha launches in the air." — [Anime News Network interview, 2024](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interview/2024-09-30/guilty-gear-artistic-identity-and-upcoming-anime-with-ken-miyauchi-shoichi-kitazono-and-genki-mamada/.214138)
- Players sometimes avoid wall breaks because the reset is "not worth it" and deliberately drop combos to keep the opponent cornered. — [Gematsu, Developer's Backyard vol. 2 (summary)](https://www.gematsu.com/2020/07/guilty-gear-strive-developers-backyard-volume-two)
- **Arc's limited-animation method (GDC 2015, Junya C. Motomura, "GuiltyGearXrd's Art Style: The X Factor Between 2D and 3D"):** they used "limited animation" with fewer, held frames and no interpolation, because full animation "made the visuals appear more 3D". They deformed the mesh at every keyframe to add 2D-like imperfections, using custom cel shaders and models built for those shaders. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The); [Arc System Works post](https://www.arcsystemworks.com/guilty-gear-xrd-art-talk-at-game-developers-conference-2015/); [Motomura slides PDF](https://www.ggxrd.com/Motomura_Junya_GuiltyGearXrd.pdf)
- **God of War (2018), the single-shot camera as a contrasting approach:** Barlog said the game is not a true continuous shot and used "six to eight tricks". He argued it gives "a sense of immediacy and connection to these characters". About 40% of the team doubted it until they played the finished game. Area changes are reportedly bridged by deaths and occasional flashes to white. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/barlog-i-god-of-war-i-s-single-shot-camera-trick-was-a-tough-sell-for-devs); flash-to-white detail via search summary of [GINX](https://www.ginx.tv/en/?p=37472)

### Inferences
- **[Observation]** Strive Wall Break shot grammar:
  1. The victim is pinned against an invisible wall that visibly cracks with each hit.
  2. On the break, the camera swings out to a dramatic angle and the victim is blasted through.
  3. Both fighters tumble and travel quickly through a short "in-between" stretch of scenery, such as falling past a cliff, through a building or across a rooftop.
  4. The fight lands in the next section of the same stage. The next section is new background art, often with a different scale, height or time of day, while the fighters stay in the same plane.

  The travel is short, violent and driven by the victim. The attacker follows, but nobody performs a graceful jump.
- **[Observation]** Granblue Fantasy Versus/Rising (Arc System Works) Skybound Arts and Granblue Fantasy: Relink's Link Attacks and Chain Bursts use the same "hit-confirm then cinematic" structure. Relink's large boss fights shift the arena with scripted events, such as platforms collapsing or the boss moving the fight to a new tier.
- **[Observation]** Genshin Impact Elemental Bursts are very short close-up camera cuts (about 1 to 2 seconds) that return straight to gameplay. Honkai: Star Rail ultimates freeze the turn order and play a longer authored cinematic, often in a stylised backdrop. Both show the convention that a signature move earns a camera takeover, then hard-cuts back.
- **[Observation]** Bayonetta (PlatinumGames) stages its set-piece fights on moving or collapsing platforms: the prologue fight on falling clock-tower debris, Bayonetta 2's opening on a fighter jet over the city, Bayonetta 1's chapter on a missile. The location keeps changing because the surface moves through the world while the fighters keep footing on it. Devil May Cry 5's Dante vs. Vergil takes place on the top of the Qliphoth, with a cutscene-framed approach.
- **[Observation]** Kingdom Hearts Dream Drop Distance's "Flowmotion" and KH3's "Attraction Flow" and athletic flow use rail-grinding, pole-swinging and wall-kicking. Traversal reads as fast because the character is always in contact with a surface or follows a fixed spline at high speed. KH2's Reaction Commands, such as the Xemnas skyscraper run, are cinematic QTE moves inside a fight.
- **[Observation]** What all these references share for weight, and what to apply to the trailer:
  - **Anticipation:** a visible crouch or coil before any leap, held 3 to 6 frames at 24 fps.
  - **Fast, flat travel:** a short arc or a straight line, never a slow parabola.
  - **Contact:** feet plant with dust, debris or a ripple. Landings compress (knees bend, a hand touches the ground) and slide or skid.
  - **Hit-stop:** a few frames of freeze on the clash, plus camera shake.
  - **Smears, speed lines and afterimages:** these replace in-between frames.
  - **Limited animation:** animating on 2s or 3s with held key poses, which Arc documents as making 3D look 2D and punchier.
  - **Camera:** the camera follows the victim's body through a knock-through, or hard-cuts on impact (match-on-action), instead of floating alongside a jumper.

- No sources were retrieved this session for Kingdom Hearts, Genshin, Honkai Star Rail, Bayonetta, Devil May Cry or Granblue Relink. All points about them above are unverified observations.

## 6. Cross-cutting: what makes movement feel weighty, not floaty (timing, anticipation, landings, surface contact, camera)

### Takeaway
None of the games documented here move a duel between locations with a long voluntary jump. They use one of five devices: a phase-break cutscene or QTE (CyberConnect2), a knock-through driven by the victim (Strive, DBFZ), field replacement (Domain Expansion), a fight on a moving surface (Bayonetta), or staying on the ground in one scenic place and escalating through weather and lighting (Ghost, Sekiro). Weight comes from limited, held animation, hit-stop, surface contact and a camera that either follows impact or cuts on it.

### Cited Findings
- Arc System Works deliberately chose limited animation (fewer, held frames) over full animation because full animation made characters "appear more 3D". Per-keyframe mesh deformation was used to sell 2D-style impact. — [GDC Vault, Motomura 2015](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)
- Strive's transitions are seamless (no loading cut) and integrated with the fighting mechanic, so the change of location is the reward for a hit. — [ANN interview](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interview/2024-09-30/guilty-gear-artistic-identity-and-upcoming-anime-with-ken-miyauchi-shoichi-kitazono-and-genki-mamada/.214138); [guiltygear.com](https://www.guiltygear.com/ggst/en/battle/systems/)
- CyberConnect2's boss fights use cutscenes plus QTE at phase breaks and at the finish. — [Wikipedia: Storm 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto_Shippuden:_Ultimate_Ninja_Storm_2); [Wikipedia: Storm 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto_Shippuden:_Ultimate_Ninja_Storm_3); [Steam Deck HQ, Hinokami](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/demon-slayer-the-hinokami-chronicles/)
- Ghost's duel tension comes from stillness, wind and particles ("serene" before violence), with framing drawn from Kurosawa's wide compositions. — [GameRant](https://gamerant.com/ghost-tsushima-duels/); [VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/creating-the-way-of-the-warrior-for-ghost-of-tsushima/); [Flicks](https://www.flicks.com.au/features/how-the-hit-ps4-game-ghost-of-tsushima-was-influenced-by-kurosawa-and-samurai-cinema/)
- God of War shows the opposite approach: an apparently unbroken camera, achieved with "six to eight tricks" and flash-to-white bridges. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/barlog-i-god-of-war-i-s-single-shot-camera-trick-was-a-tough-sell-for-devs)

### Inferences
- A possible transition map for the four trailer locations, combining the documented devices:
  1. **Temple courtyard to rainy Taisho street:** a Strive or DBFZ-style knock-through. A heavy clash blasts one fighter through the temple gate or wall. The camera follows the body, with a brief tumble through a stylised in-between (an ink-wash streak or a torii flash), and hard-lands them skidding on wet cobbles. Splash, sparks off the blade dragged on stone, and the rain starts on the cut.
  2. **Rainy street to burning rooftops:** a Naruto or KH-style surface chase. Short contact hops up awnings, a wall-run up a building face with the camera tilted so the wall reads as the floor, then tile-to-tile sprinting where each footfall cracks or kicks up tiles. Embers and the fire glow are introduced as they crest the roofline.
  3. **Burning rooftops to infinite castle:** field replacement, as with Domain Expansion or Hinokami's Infinite Castle-style framing. The fighters do not travel. On a clash freeze-frame, the world folds or swaps around them: fusuma doors slam in from all sides and the floor tilts. Alternatively, the roof collapses and they fall into the castle in one straight, fast vertical drop with debris (Bayonetta-style moving surface), landing in a compressed crouch.
  4. **Finale inside the castle:** a CyberConnect2-style cinematic QTE finish. The most authored, storyboard-faithful shots go here.
- Rules for weight, applied per shot:
  - Never show a jump longer than about 8 to 12 frames of airtime without an impact, cut or smear.
  - Every takeoff gets a coil; every landing gets compression, a skid and debris.
  - Speed is shown with afterimages, smear frames and straight-line dashes, not arcs.
  - Animate on 2s or 3s with holds on key poses.
  - Give every blade contact 2 to 6 frames of hit-stop plus camera shake and a spark burst.
  - Cut on impact (match-on-action), not mid-air.
- These timing numbers (frames of hit-stop, airtime limits) are the researcher's heuristics from animation practice, not values from any source retrieved here.

### Gaps

- No CyberConnect2 CEDEC talk on combat camera or anime reproduction was found.
- No developer source was found explaining why long jumps read as floaty. That conclusion is inferred from the absence of such jumps in the documented techniques and from general animation principles.
