# Anime films and series: carrying one sword/martial-arts duel across many locations without "floaty" movement

Scope note for the report writer: the official, citable production record on *staging technique* (as opposed to praise) is thin. Most English-language interviews with ufotable (Sotozaki, Terao) focus on 3D/2D integration and render scale, not on how a fight is routed between spaces. Where I describe what happens on screen in a named scene and could not confirm it with a source this session, it sits under **Inferences** and is labelled **[observation, unverified]**. Please treat those as viewing leads to confirm against the episode, not as documented fact. Episode numbers marked that way come from memory and should be checked on a wiki before being quoted.

Research budget: about 18 tool calls. Sakugabooru blog posts on Demon Slayer and Fate fights did not show up in search. No YouTube video-essay titles were verified, so none are cited (see Gaps).

## 1. Demon Slayer (ufotable): how the fight moves between spaces (Mugen Train, Entertainment District, Swordsmith Village, Hashira Training, Infinity Castle 2025)

### Takeaway
What ufotable has said officially about Demon Slayer is mostly about one problem: 2D hand-drawn fighters inside fully 3D environments that a camera can travel through. They built the whole Infinity Castle as a navigable 3D set, grew it from about 100 m x 100 m to about 2 km², and say the camera and the drawn character must match exactly "or they'll slide around." The routing devices themselves (knockbacks through walls, falling through the castle, doors opening onto new rooms) are not described in interviews. They are visible on screen, so they are listed as observations.

### Cited Findings
- Yuichi Terao (ufotable Director of Photography / digital team chief) says the Infinity Castle has appeared three times in the TV/film continuity. It first appeared in TV Episode 26, the end of season 1, which he called "a very impactful scene." It appeared again in the first episode of Swordsmith Village (overall Episode 45). The 2025 film is the third appearance, and it would "really kind of explore this space." — [Popverse, Terao interview "making the Infinity Castle feel infinite"](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-yuichi-terao-interview-making-the-infinity-castle-feel-infinite)
- Terao on scale: "Back in episode 26, the maximum we were able to kind of express using 3D was about a 100 meter by 100 meter space." By Swordsmith Village they could "visually paint a background" of about 2 km². For the film, rendering speed rose "tenfold," and his stated goal was to make the castle look "almost" infinite. — [Popverse](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-yuichi-terao-interview-making-the-infinity-castle-feel-infinite)
- Popverse's descriptions of "endless hallways," "gravity-defying demons" and a "spinning camera" are the writer's own words, not Terao's. — [Popverse](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-yuichi-terao-interview-making-the-infinity-castle-feel-infinite)
- Terao on why drawn characters can look like they are floating or skating in 3D sets: "Now we're dealing with 3D backgrounds and environments, but 2D hand-drawn characters that are animated. So, we have to make sure the camera and the character's animation match perfectly or they'll slide around." He also said the whole castle had to be built in 3D because many scenes render everything except the characters in 3D. — summarised in search results attributed to Deadline/Popverse interviews; see [Popverse interview index](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-infinity-castle-ufotable-dynamic-anime-honors-koyoharu-gotouge) and [Cartoon Brew director/DP interview](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/anime/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-director-dp-interview-257634.html). (I did not open the Deadline piece itself. The quote reached me through a search summary, so check the wording before quoting it verbatim.)
- Sotozaki told Entertainment Weekly that keeping the 3D environments and the animation in sync was the most complicated part. Keyframes were tested inside the 3D environments before key animation was finished. — reported in search summaries of EW coverage; related coverage at [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-anime-ufotable-trilogy-akaza-tanjiro-2000703501) (secondary)
- Sotozaki says four people at ufotable led the final Tanjiro and Giyu vs Akaza fight, and key animator Masayuki Kunihiro handled about 80% of it. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-anime-ufotable-trilogy-akaza-tanjiro-2000703501)
- Sotozaki says he focused on making viewers feel dropped into the castle along with the Demon Slayer Corps at the film's opening. — search summary of Popverse/AWN coverage ([AWN tag page](https://awn.com/tag/koyoharu-gotouge)) (secondary; I did not read the original page)
- About 2,200 cuts. Hand-drawn staff outnumbered 3D staff by more than five to one, with 3D camera work and effects layered onto 2D animation to build depth. — promotional/aggregator source summarised in search; treat with caution ([mario-mag blog](https://mario-mag.blogspot.com/2025/12/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-team.html), low reliability)
- In the Cartoon Brew interview, Sotozaki and Terao talk only in general terms ("the action sequences are always going to be difficult"; "a spiral where expectations continue to grow"). It has nothing on the castle fall, the rooms or the camera. — [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/anime/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-director-dp-interview-257634.html)
- Entertainment District Episode 10 ("Never Give Up," aired 6 Feb 2022) is widely called the series' animation peak. Anime Corner's write-up describes plot beats (Zenitsu bursting "out of the rubble" with Godspeed, Tengen cutting Gyutaro's hand, the simultaneous beheadings) but names no animators and no staging techniques. — [Anime Corner](https://animecorner.me/demon-slayer-entertainment-district-arc-episode-10-insane-animation-for-the-climax/); [Rotten Tomatoes air date](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/tv/demon_slayer_kimetsu_no_yaiba/s03/e10)
- ufotable published storyboards for the Entertainment District fight (Tanjiro, Inosuke, Zenitsu, Tengen). These are a primary visual source for how cuts are boarded, but the article names no artists. — [ComicBook.com](https://comicbook.com/anime/news/demon-slayer-season-2-behind-the-scenes-storyboarding)

### Inferences
**Routing devices visible on screen [observation, unverified; confirm by viewing]:**
- **Infinity Castle as a set that moves itself (film 2025, and TV ep 26 / Swordsmith ep 1).** Nakime's biwa strike works as an in-world cut motivation: a twang and the room rearranges. Shoji doors slide open onto a different room, floors drop away, and rooms rotate or stack vertically. When the film opens, the Corps fall *through* the castle from Ubuyashiki's mansion. The camera falls with them past stacked floors, verandas and inverted buildings. That gives "a vertical move" without anyone jumping, because gravity does the work and the bodies tumble limply rather than gliding. Each solo fight (Shinobu vs Doma, Zenitsu vs Kaigaku, Tanjiro and Giyu vs Akaza) then lands in its own room, which reads as a "location." For the trailer's surreal castle, the lesson is that the *environment* carries the fighters, so they never have to leap.
- **Mugen Train (2020).** The fight with Enmu happens on top of a moving train at night. The train roof is a treadmill, so horizontal travel comes from the vehicle rather than from jumping. Tanjiro and Inosuke run and crouch against the wind on the carriage roofs, and Inosuke runs along the outside of the cars. After the derailment the fight changes location by wreckage: the train crashes and the scene resets to the ground beside it. Rengoku vs Akaza at dawn stays mostly *grounded* in a field by the wreck. Its sense of travel comes from fast ground dashes, ground-skimming camera moves and earth thrown up by each step, not from aerial arcs. Akaza's few leaps are short and end in heavy impacts. Dawn (the light rising) acts as a timer and a lighting change rather than a location change.
- **Entertainment District arc (2021–22).** The fight escalates *upward and outward* through the district. It starts in interiors (the Ogimoto, Kyogoku and Tokito houses), moves to the street, then to the rooftops. Gyutaro's flying blood sickles and Daki's obi slice whole rows of buildings, so destruction opens new sightlines and turns standing streets into rubble fields across the episodes. Zenitsu's Thunderclap is a straight-line dash that crosses a large distance in one cut, ending in a slide and debris rather than a hang in the air. Characters are knocked through walls and roofs and land in rubble, and the next scene picks them up digging out "of the rubble," as Anime Corner's own beat description puts it. That is the "thrown out of frame, land somewhere new" device.
- **Swordsmith Village (2023).** The Hantengu and Gyokko fights spread across the village, the forest and a hillside spring. Mitsuri's whip-sword lets a character cover distance with the *weapon's* reach instead of a jump. Mitsuri vs Zohakuten (episode 7 or 8 of the arc) uses a giant wooden dragon construct and a clifftop, so the vertical drama is in the opponent's mass rather than in the hero floating.
- **Hashira Training (2024).** Mostly training scenes. The finale (Muzan attacking Ubuyashiki) leads directly into the castle fall, so the arc's last shot is the "trapdoor" transition that opens the film.
- **General ufotable pattern.** 3D camera dollies or rotates around 2D fighters while the set itself moves. Per Terao, if the drawn feet do not lock to the 3D ground plane, the character "slides." The anti-float rule is therefore partly *technical*: feet must be pinned to surfaces in the same camera space.

### Gaps

- No source found on the Mugen Train Akaza fight's camera or layout process.
- No Japanese-language production notes (pamphlets, CGWorld, WebNewtype) were reached this session. CGWorld is likely to have Infinity Castle 3D-layout specifics.
- I could not confirm whether Sotozaki or Terao have spoken about Nakime's biwa as an editing device.

## 2. Fate/stay night: Unlimited Blade Works and Heaven's Feel (ufotable): rooftop and building-to-building fights, falls and wall kicks

### Takeaway
Little was documented in the sources I reached. Terao's own interview covers lighting and compositing (characters "blend into the backgrounds" when lit correctly) and calls one chase sequence a CG milestone. Specific staging of Archer vs Lancer or Saber vs Berserker is not covered in any source I found.

### Cited Findings
- Terao on Heaven's Feel I backgrounds: "it was more time-consuming to get Heaven's Feel's backgrounds right than for any other project we've done." — [ANN, Interview: Yuichi Terao (2018)](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2018-01-10/interview-yuichi-terao-chief-of-ufotable-digital-team/.125658)
- Terao: "if you do the lighting correctly, the characters will blend into the backgrounds no matter what style they're drawn in." This bears directly on grounding: a figure that is not lit like its environment reads as pasted on and floating. — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2018-01-10/interview-yuichi-terao-chief-of-ufotable-digital-team/.125658)
- Terao: "the chase scene on the highway represented a big step for ufotable's CG photography team." — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2018-01-10/interview-yuichi-terao-chief-of-ufotable-digital-team/.125658)
- Terao's philosophy: "The more you strive for realism, the more contrived it appears." He prefers "exaggerating the beauty within the scenery and within individual shots." — [ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2018-01-10/interview-yuichi-terao-chief-of-ufotable-digital-team/.125658)
- UBW (2014) opened with a double-length prologue episode. Much of the Fate/Zero staff returned. — [Anime Herald](https://www.animeherald.com/2014/10/08/fatestay-night-unlimited-blade-works-gorgeous-grail-war/); [Wikipedia episode list (for placement only)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fate/Stay_Night:_Unlimited_Blade_Works_episodes)

### Inferences
- **UBW Prologue (#00): Archer vs Lancer in the school grounds at night [observation, unverified].** The duel stays on the ground in a courtyard. Spear and twin blades trade at close range, sparks give the camera light sources, and both fighters skid back on the ground after each exchange, with no aerial arcs. Shirou witnessing it and fleeing pushes the scene to a new location (into the school building, then a corridor), so the *chase* relocates the fight. When Lancer pursues, he appears inside the building rather than being shown jumping there.
- **UBW #01: Saber vs Berserker on a night street near the bridge or cemetery [observation, unverified].** Berserker's landings crater the asphalt. His mass is sold by debris and camera shake, and Saber is shown being pushed back along the ground in long skids. Archer fires from a distant rooftop, so a third location is joined in by *cross-cutting* to the shooter rather than by anyone travelling.
- **Heaven's Feel II (2019): Saber Alter vs Berserker in the Einzbern forest [observation, unverified].** This is the most-cited example of ufotable's tracking camera through 3D trees around 2D fighters. Berserker kicks off trunks and trees splinter on contact, so the vertical movement always ends on a surface. Footholds are trees and rocks, not air.
- **Heaven's Feel III (2020): Rider vs Saber Alter [observation, unverified].** Rider runs *along* walls and building facades (parkour, using chains as anchors) instead of jumping across open space. Chains and anchors are one way to justify long aerial travel: a tether reads as being pulled, not as floating.
- The ANN quote does not say which film the "highway chase" is in. Heaven's Feel I was the film in release at the time of that interview, but this is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No sakuga breakdown or staff interview on the UBW Archer vs Lancer or Saber vs Berserker staging was found. Fan pages (TV Tropes, MAL) offered only opinion.
- No source on wall-kick or fall staging in UBW Episode 12 or 20 (Archer vs Shirou, Archer vs Berserker) or in the Heaven's Feel films.

## 3. Bleach TYBW, Jujutsu Kaisen, Chainsaw Man, Mob Psycho 100, Naruto Shippuden, SAO: aerial combat and location changes

### Takeaway
The best documented pieces are these: JJK Season 2 anchors Shibuya action to real, filmed physical locations, where "environments are inseparable from the setpieces." MAPPA's Chainsaw Man Reze film built a "Chainsaw Man blown away" shot as a 3D camera movie at the layout stage before drawing over it. Bleach TYBW shot its own reference photos and video for hard angles. Flash-step and free-fall specifics were not documented in the sources I reached.

### Inferences
- **Bleach TYBW flash step (shunpo) [observation, unverified].** Flash step is staged as a cut, not as a motion: a character is in pose A, a smear or afterimage flickers, and they are in pose B somewhere else. That avoids airtime entirely. In a trailer it lets a fighter "teleport" from courtyard to street with an in-world justification and no floaty arc. The TYBW opening episodes (Yamamoto vs Yhwach, Ichigo vs Quilge, the Wandenreich invasion) use full-screen negative/inverted colour frames on hits, which double as hard cuts.
- **JJK Shibuya [observation, unverified].** Yuji vs Choso (S2 Ep 13) and Yuji and Todo vs Mahito (S2 Ep 21–22) move *through floors and down stairwells* of the real station rather than over it. Vertical movement is falls, slides down escalators and drops through broken floors, always with debris. Sukuna vs Mahoraga (S2 Ep 17) is a city-scale fight where both fighters are punched through buildings and a cut picks them up in a new block.
- **Naruto Shippuden [observation, unverified].** Pain vs Naruto (Ep 166–167, Konoha crater) and Kakashi vs Pain (Ep 157–158) use rooftop running in the series' classic "leaf village rooftop hop" style. Each hop is short, low and lands on a tile with a dust puff, so travel reads as running, not flying. Ep 167's animators and director were not verified this session.
- **Sword Art Online [observation, unverified].** Alfheim's flight combat (S1 second half) and Alicization's aerial fights deliberately *are* flight, enabled by wings. They are a useful negative example: when a world grants flight, floating is intended, so a grounded duel should avoid looking like SAO's Alfheim.
- **Mob Psycho [observation].** Psychic fights justify aerial movement through visible force (auras, debris lifted around the body), which turns floating into a power display rather than a weightless drift.

## 4. How aerial movement is sold so it doesn't read as floating

### Takeaway
The documented core is technical. Drawn feet must lock to the 3D ground plane and camera (Terao: or "they'll slide around"), and characters must be lit like their environment to look embedded (Terao, ANN). Fights grounded in physical, real environments read with weight (JJK Ep 13 notes). The rest of the craft vocabulary (anticipation, contact, short airtime, debris) is standard animation practice and visible in these shows, but I did not find it stated in a studio source this session.

### Cited Findings
- Camera and drawn character must match, or characters "slide around." This is foot-lock as the anti-float baseline. — Terao via [Popverse](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-yuichi-terao-interview-making-the-infinity-castle-feel-infinite) / Deadline summary (see section 1 caveat)
- Correct lighting makes characters "blend into the backgrounds." Mismatched light is a cause of the "pasted on, floating" look. — [ANN Terao](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2018-01-10/interview-yuichi-terao-chief-of-ufotable-digital-team/.125658)
- Ufotable tested keyframes inside the 3D environments before finishing key animation (Sotozaki to EW, via search summary). — [Gizmodo (related)](https://gizmodo.com/demon-slayer-infinity-castle-anime-ufotable-trilogy-akaza-tanjiro-2000703501)
- MAPPA blocked a body being blown away as a 3D camera movie first, so the arc, speed and camera follow were decided physically before drawing. — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/see-how-denji-vs-reze-fight-animation-sequence-from-chainsaw-man-the-movie-reze-arc-was-turned-from-3d-to-2d)
- Weight in a Mob Psycho II scene comes from a "feeling of volumetry" as a figure edges toward the camera, meaning volume and depth movement rather than flat translation. — [Sakugabooru Blog](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2019/01/26/mob-psycho-100-ii-production-notes-3/)
- Pierrot used live reference photos and video for hard angles in Bleach TYBW. — [Oricon](https://us.oricon-group.com/news/2334/)

### Inferences (a checklist built from observation of the shows above, not from studio statements)
1. **Anticipation and push-off.** A visible crouch or wind-up of 3–6 frames, then the take-off drawn with a smear. Ground cracks or tiles pop at the take-off point. Without the push, a jump reads as levitation. [observation]
2. **Short airtime, long ground travel.** Demon Slayer's Thunderclap (Zenitsu) and Rengoku/Akaza dashes cover distance *horizontally and close to the ground*, often ending in a slide that ploughs dirt. Anime fights rarely show a full parabolic arc. They show take-off, cut, and landing. [observation]
3. **Surface contact every beat.** Wall runs (Rider, HF3), kicks off tree trunks (Berserker, HF2), roof-tile landings (Naruto rooftop hops, the Entertainment District), and landing on poles and edges. The character is always touching something or about to. [observation]
4. **Heavy landings.** A crouch on landing that compresses the body. Debris, dust and a camera shake on impact. Craters for heavy characters (Berserker, Akaza). [observation]
5. **The camera travels with the body.** The 3D camera tracks or dollies with the fighter, so the background streaks and the character stays roughly framed. Speed reads from the environment moving, not from the character drifting across a static frame. Ufotable's 3D sets exist largely to allow this. [observation, consistent with Terao's statements]
6. **Gravity as the vertical engine.** Make vertical moves *falls*, not jumps: the Infinity Castle drop, crashing through floors (JJK Shibuya), sliding down roofs. Falling bodies tumble and flail, and that loss of control reads as weight. [observation]
7. **Tethers and reach.** Chains (Rider), whip-swords (Mitsuri) and the weapon's reach let a fighter cover a gap while being pulled or extending, never drifting. [observation]
8. **Slow-motion hang time saved for the climax only.** The few held mid-air frames in Demon Slayer come at decisive strikes (for example the final beheadings in Entertainment District Ep 10, and Akaza's last moments in the Infinity Castle film). If every jump hangs, nothing reads as special and everything floats. [observation]
9. **Lighting embedding.** Rim light from the environment (fire, neon, lanterns) on the fighter. Terao's lighting point applies directly to rooftops on fire and a rainy neon street. [cited principle + observation]

## 5. Edit and transition devices that carry a fight to a new place

### Takeaway
The only device documented in my sources is environmental and lighting change caused by destruction (JJK Ep 13's neons). Ufotable's in-world architecture changes (the Infinity Castle) are documented as a 3D space the camera explores, but not as an editing device. The other devices (knockback-through-wall cuts, flash-step cuts, impact frames, white-outs, falls that drop into a new location) are recurring on-screen patterns in the named shows.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences (device catalogue from on-screen observation, mapped to the trailer's four locations)
- **Knockback and land somewhere new.** A hit sends one fighter out of frame. Cut to the new location as they crash in, through a wall, roof or shoji, already moving with debris. The audience fills in the travel. Seen in the Entertainment District (bodies through house walls, then "out of the rubble"), JJK Shibuya (through floors) and Sukuna vs Mahoraga (through buildings). [observation] *Trailer use: temple courtyard to rainy street, with a kick through the temple gate or wall that lands them skidding on wet cobbles.*
- **A fall as the transition.** A floor collapses or a roof caves in, and the camera falls with the character. Cut on the fall to a new location. Infinity Castle opening; JJK Shibuya station floors. [observation] *Trailer use: burning rooftops into infinite castle, as the roof gives way and the fall continues into an impossible castle drop.*
- **The environment moves, not the fighters.** Nakime's biwa strike makes doors slide open onto a new room and the floor rotate. Mugen Train's moving roof. [observation] *Trailer use: inside the castle, shoji slide open between strikes so each clash happens in a new room with no travel.*
- **Match cut on motion or a sword arc.** A horizontal slash in location A cuts on the swing to the follow-through in location B. Demon Slayer's breathing-style water and flame effects often fill the frame as they sweep, creating natural wipe points. [observation; not confirmed for a specific cut]
- **Impact frame or white-out.** A full-frame flash, inverted colour or a single held impact drawing on the strongest hit, used as a hard cut. Bleach TYBW's negative frames and Demon Slayer's flash on clashing blades are examples. [observation]
- **Flash-step cut.** An afterimage or smear, then a hard cut to the fighter repositioned (Bleach shunpo, Zenitsu's Thunderclap). [observation]
- **A chase that relocates the fight.** One fighter breaks off and the other pursues. Cut to them arriving in the new space mid-run, as in UBW Prologue (Lancer pursuing Shirou into the school). [observation]
- **A continuous 3D camera ("one-take").** Ufotable's 3D sets let the camera fly continuously through an environment (the Infinity Castle; HF2's forest tracking). This works for *within-location* continuity, while hard cuts handle *between-location* jumps. [inferred from Terao's statements + observation]
- **Destruction or lighting as the scene change.** Breaking the light source changes the visual location without moving (JJK Ep 13). *Trailer use: rainy street to burning rooftops, as a slash cuts a paper lantern or gas line, fire catches, and the next shot is rooftops already ablaze.* [cited device + inference]
