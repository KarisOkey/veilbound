# Cut Between Worlds, Never Float Between Them

Anime and anime-style fighting games almost never move a duel to a new place with a long, visible jump. They change location **on the cut or through the world**. A hit drives one fighter through scenery and the camera follows the body. A motion starts in place A and finishes in place B. A flash or impact frame hides the seam. Or the set itself moves, collapses or is replaced around the fighters. The jumps that do appear on screen are short, fast and asymmetric: the fall is quicker than the rise, every take-off and landing is a contact event with dust, debris, a held pose and hit-stop, and the camera locks to the ground at both ends instead of riding the bodies through the sky. Yutaka Nakamura, the most influential action animator in the field, called one of his own scenes "a bit too much" because a fast camera pull made the characters "seem to fly rather than run" ([Wave Motion Cannon](https://wavemotioncannon.com/2016/11/02/interview-yutaka-nakamura-web-anime-style-7192003-part-2/)). That is the "two people floating" problem in one sentence. A check of `game/combat.js` shows the current trailer leap is close to the worst case: both fighters take off together on a symmetric parabola lasting up to 2.1 seconds, with an eased start and finish that slows them at both ends. The fix is mostly editorial and cheap. Delete the on-screen two-person leaps, replace each location change with a knock-through, a match-on-action cut or a world change, and rebuild the remaining jumps as short, contact-heavy hops. All of this can be done procedurally with the effects already in `anime-fx.js`.

## Five causes turn a leap into a float

The research converges on five causes of floatiness. The first is real or low gravity with a symmetric rise and fall. At 9.81 m/s², a 6 m leap takes about 1.1 s to reach its apex, so roughly 2.2 s of airtime with nothing happening. Games instead design jumps from apex height and time-to-apex (g = 2h/t², v₀ = 2h/t), the method from Kyle Pittman's GDC 2016 talk as summarised by [GDQuest](https://www.gdquest.com/library/kinematic_jump_formulas). They make the fall much faster than the rise: in **Super Mario Bros. falling gravity is 3.2 to 3.6 times rising gravity** ([SMBpedia](https://simplistic6502.github.io/smb1_tll/smbpedia_movement.html)). The second cause is smooth, evenly interpolated motion. Arc System Works told GDC 2015 that full, interpolated animation made Guilty Gear Xrd's characters "appear more 3D", so they switched to **held, stepped keys** ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)). The third is missing contact events: no crouch, no push-off, no landing compression, no dust. The fourth is a camera moving faster than the bodies' contact cues, which is Nakamura's warning. The fifth is showing the whole parabola uninterrupted when anime would cut into it.

The current build hits all five. In `game/combat.js`, `leapTo()` sends both fighters along `arc = 4·h·u·(1−u)`, a perfectly symmetric parabola. Horizontal travel uses an ease-in-out curve, so the pair decelerates near take-off and landing, the slowest and most visible moments. In `game/trailer.js`, `doLeap()` sets durations of **1.15 to 2.1 s** with heights of at least 2.6 m. The jump animation fades in over 0.06 s with no anticipation crouch. The two fighters leave the ground on the same frame and fly matching arcs (their apex heights differ by only 0.35 m), which reads as one rigid formation rather than two bodies with mass. Every world also contains a scheduled `'leap'` beat, so the weakest motion in the trailer repeats four times.

ufotable treats grounding as partly a camera-matching problem and partly a lighting problem. Yuichi Terao, its director of photography, says drawn characters in 3D sets must match the camera exactly "or they'll slide around" ([Popverse](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-yuichi-terao-interview-making-the-infinity-castle-feel-infinite); wording reached the researchers through a search summary). He also says that "if you do the lighting correctly, the characters will blend into the backgrounds no matter what style they're drawn in" ([ANN](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2018-01-10/interview-yuichi-terao-chief-of-ufotable-digital-team/.125658)). For code-built fighters, that means a contact shadow that shrinks and fades with height and never disappears, plus rim light taken from each location's own key light: lanterns in the rain, fire on the rooftops.

## The location changes on the cut, not in the air

Every documented source routes fights between spaces in one of five ways, and none of them is a voluntary long jump.

**The knock-through** is the best documented. Guilty Gear Strive's Wall Break turns sustained pressure at the stage edge into a transition to a new area ([official battle system page](https://www.guiltygear.com/ggst/en/battle/systems/)). Genki Mamada of Arc System Works says the team "wanted Strive to be seamless—that there's no cut-off", integrating "the background and stage transitions with the fighting mechanic" ([ANN, 2024](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interview/2024-09-30/guilty-gear-artistic-identity-and-upcoming-anime-with-ken-miyauchi-shoichi-kitazono-and-genki-mamada/.214138)). Director Akira Katano tuned it so "the player will celebrate breaking the wall rather than feel disappointed" ([Unreal Engine interview excerpt](https://www.unrealengine.com/en-US/developer-interviews/how-guilty-gear--strive--hits-an-ultra-combo-with-groundbreaking-visuals-and-gameplay)). The victim first slams the wall and "slowly slide[s] down" before the break ([Niche Gamer, summarising Famitsu](https://nichegamer.com/guilty-gear-strive-directors-insist-i-no-was-not-censored-but-more-accessible-along-with-gameplay/)). Dragon Ball FighterZ ties its Dramatic Finishes to destroyed stage states ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103042/dragon-ball-fighterz---how-to-do-dramatic-finishes-and-dramatic-intros)). Sparking! Zero's reveal trailer sells "massive and destructible fighting arenas" by launching Goku into a rock formation ([Bandai Namco](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/pt_br/news/dragon-ball-sparking-zero-announcement)). In anime, Demon Slayer's Entertainment District climax picks Zenitsu up bursting "out of the rubble" after the fight has torn through the district ([Anime Corner](https://animecorner.me/demon-slayer-entertainment-district-arc-episode-10-insane-animation-for-the-climax/)). The key property is that **travel is driven by the victim**. A body thrown by a hit reads as violent displacement, never as flight.

**The phase break** is CyberConnect2's method. Naruto Storm and Hinokami Chronicles boss fights alternate arena combat with cutscenes and quick-time events, and finish on a cinematic QTE that delivers the final blow ([Wikipedia: Storm 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto_Shippuden:_Ultimate_Ninja_Storm_2); [Steam Deck HQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/demon-slayer-the-hinokami-chronicles/)). Reviewers single out these boss fights as the best part of Hinokami 2 ([Game8 review index](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-the-hinokami-chronicles-2-review)). CyberConnect2 removed them from Storm Generations to make it "more like a fighting game" ([Gematsu, summary](https://www.gematsu.com/2012/10/interview-cyberconnect2-ceo-hiroshi-matsuyama-on-narutimate-storm-3-hack-and-jojos-bizarre-adventure)), which confirms they are a deliberate cinematic layer on top of the fighter. Where the location changes, it changes at the break, by a hard cut.

**Field replacement** swaps the world around stationary fighters. Jujutsu Kaisen: Cursed Clash's Domain Expansions "evolve the entire field", and its stages include "gimmicks that cause the stage to collapse when attacked" ([Bandai Namco](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/games/jujutsu-kaisen-cursed-clash)). The game reviewed badly ([Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/jujutsu-kaisen-cursed-clash)), so it is a reference for the concept, not the execution. The same idea scales down to lighting. In Jujutsu Kaisen S2 episode 13, the neon lighting "only kick[s] in when Choso destroys the regular lights", so destruction changes the visual location without anyone moving ([Sakuga Blog](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2023/10/24/creators-rebelling-against-the-odds-jujutsu-kaisen-season-2-production-notes-1-13/)).

**The environment that moves.** ufotable built the Infinity Castle as a navigable 3D set. It grew from about 100 m × 100 m in TV episode 26 to about 2 km² by Swordsmith Village, with tenfold rendering speed for the 2025 film, so the camera could "really kind of explore this space" ([Popverse](https://www.thepopverse.com/movies-demon-slayer-kimetsu-no-yaiba-yuichi-terao-interview-making-the-infinity-castle-feel-infinite)). The team placed cameras at many angles and tested to find the most intense shot. Director Haruo Sotozaki reportedly aimed to make viewers feel dropped into the castle along with the Demon Slayer Corps ([AWN coverage, secondary](https://awn.com/tag/koyoharu-gotouge)). For a surreal castle this is the whole lesson: the architecture carries the fighters, so they never need to leap. The researchers also list well-known on-screen devices, such as Nakime's biwa rearranging rooms and the Corps falling through stacked floors. These are viewing leads that no source confirmed, and they should be checked against the film before anyone quotes them.

**The editorial cut** is film grammar. In The Graduate, Benjamin pulls himself onto a pool raft and lands, across the cut, on a hotel bed. That is a single motion started in one place and finished in another ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-match-on-action-cut/)). God of War (2018) presents an unbroken camera, but Cory Barlog admits to "six to eight tricks", and area changes hide behind deaths and an occasional flash to white ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/barlog-i-god-of-war-i-s-single-shot-camera-trick-was-a-tough-sell-for-devs); [Critics at Large](https://www.criticsatlarge.ca/2018/05/prose-poem-power-sony-santa-monicas-god.html)). Editor Paul Rogers carried Everything Everywhere All at Once's fights across universes with "a little bell ring and reverse that would lead you and then snap you into a new universe". He said much of the effect "is done with sound design" ([Frame.io](https://blog.frame.io/2022/04/13/art-of-the-cut-everything-everywhere-all-at-once/)). Walter Murch's priority order for any cut puts emotion at 51% and 3D spatial continuity last at 4% ([UT Austin wiki](https://cloud.wikis.utexas.edu/wiki/spaces/rtf318/pages/80022510/Murch+s+Rule+of+Six)). The floaty leaps protect the criterion that matters least, spatial continuity, at the expense of rhythm, which matters more.

Applied to the four locations, the devices sort naturally. The current approach ("launch into a white-out, pair drops in from the sky") keeps the white flash but adds a drop from the sky, which is another float. The table proposes a different entry for each location, so the transitions escalate rather than repeat.

| Transition | Primary device | Precedent | How it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| Courtyard → rainy street | Match on action with an audio pre-lap | The Graduate; EEAAO sound snap | Haru's downward diagonal slash starts in sunlight and finishes in rain at night, with the blade at the same screen position and direction. The rain hiss and blade ring lead the picture by 4–8 frames. |
| Rainy street → burning rooftops | Destruction-as-scene-change, then impact-frame smash cut | JJK S2 ep 13 lights; God of War white flash | A slash cuts a gas lamp or lantern line, fire flares, a 1–2 frame ink or white impact frame plays, and the next shot is mid-exchange on a burning ridge. |
| Burning rooftops → infinite castle | Knock-through plus a fall | Strive Wall Break; Infinity Castle as a moving set | Mitsuki is kicked through a burning roof panel. Fire and debris fill the frame as a wipe, and the camera falls with both fighters past impossible floors before a compressed landing. |
| Inside the castle | The environment moves | Infinity Castle; Cursed Clash Domain | Fusuma slide open between strikes so each clash happens in a "new" room with no travel. |

Every new location should open **mid-exchange**, on a blade lock or a parry spark, never on an arrival. That keeps intensity continuous across the seam.

## Weight comes from contact, asymmetry and held poses

Vertical movement still has a place, but its job changes. It should be short and fast, bracketed by contact events, and usually a fall rather than a jump. Anime's drawing-rate conventions encode this. Shingo Natsume slows the drawing rate as a robot rises in Gurren Lagann and speeds it up as it crashes down. Otsuka put titanic creatures on threes so their "slow, deliberate movements suggest their titanic power and weight". One-Punch Man holds the impact drawing longer so the hit registers ([Wave Motion Cannon](https://wavemotioncannon.com/2016/12/31/an-introduction-to-framerate-modulation/)). Celeste adds a subtle **half-gravity apex** for readability ([Maddy Thorson](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html)). The result is a slow-up, brief-hang, fast-down arc, which is the opposite of the current symmetric one.

Worked through the GDQuest formulas, a 6 m-high landmark leap over a 20 m gap with t_up = 0.35 s needs v₀ ≈ 34 m/s and rising gravity of about 98 m/s². A 0.22 s descent needs about 248 m/s², a 2.5× fall-to-rise ratio, between GDQuest's example (about 2.6×) and Mario (3.2–3.6×). Total airtime is about **0.6–0.7 s** instead of 2 s. Short rooftop hops of 1–2 m run 0.35–0.45 s. These are derived numbers, not studio values; no source gives canonical anime airtimes. The research notes add a working rule from them: anything airborne for more than about 1 s needs a mid-air event (a clash, a wall kick, a pole touch) or a cut. On screen, no uninterrupted parabola should outlast one beat of about 0.5–0.7 s.

The contact events themselves come from fighting-game feel research. Masahiro Sakurai describes hit-stop as the moment "both parties momentarily freeze, emphasizing the power of impact" ([Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)). Smash Ultimate scales it as **⌊d·0.65 + 6⌋ frames, capped at 30**, and shakes the victim during the freeze ([SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)). Vlambeer's "Art of Screenshake" adds **permanence**: debris and marks that stay ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games); [recreation devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects)). Insomniac kept Spider-Man's momentum unbroken at every contact and used the camera to show "the impact of your change in momentum" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/it-took-three-whole-years-for-insomniac-to-get-marvels-spider-mans-web-swinging-just-right)). Nakamura says "the light reactions are most important ones", meaning the recoil on the other body proves the force ([Wave Motion Cannon](https://wavemotioncannon.com/2016/11/02/interview-yutaka-nakamura-web-anime-style-7192003-part-2/)). Together these give the anatomy of a weighty jump. A crouch of 0.10–0.20 s with hips dropped via IK foot-lock comes first. A one- or two-tick push-off follows, with a dust ring, debris and a ground crack. The ascent stretches along velocity. The apex holds a real pose, such as a raised sword or tucked knees, for 0.06–0.15 s, never a neutral hang. The descent is fast, with IK reaching the feet to the landing point in the last 0.1 s. The landing compresses deeper than the crouch, with 2–4 frames of hit-stop, vertically biased shake, a decal that stays and a skid. These phase timings are the researchers' synthesis of the cited game-feel values and animation principles.

Surface contact turns travel into running instead of flying. The documented games that read as fast but grounded all keep the character touching something. Strive's victims tumble through a short stretch of scenery. Bayonetta fights on falling debris and moving vehicles. The Naruto Storm games run low and flat with short hops. Kingdom Hearts rail-grinds and wall-kicks. Only Strive's mechanic is source-documented ([guiltygear.com](https://www.guiltygear.com/ggst/en/battle/systems/)); the rest are the researchers' recollections. The procedural consequence is clear either way. One landmark leap should be broken into three to six hops of 0.35–0.45 s, each with a 0.08–0.15 s ground touch. A wall kick plants the foot on a raycast hit, holds 2–3 pose ticks, sprays debris along the wall normal and relaunches at 0.8–1.0× incoming speed. A pole or ridge landing makes the *object* wobble. A roof slide drags a hand and emits tile chips at a rate proportional to speed. Celeste allows wall jumps from 2 px away ([Maddy Thorson](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/index.html)). The equivalent for scripted fighters is a 0.3–0.5 m landing snap, so choreography never visibly misses or hovers over a surface.

Two fighters need offsets. Synchronised take-offs and matching arcs are what make the current pair look like a single rigid object. Staggering take-off by 0.1–0.25 s and giving one fighter a low, fast arc and the other a higher, slower one restores two separate masses. That is the researchers' inference, consistent with how Strive keeps the attacker following a victim who is driven through the scenery.

Weight also comes from the clash. Ghost of Tsushima's duels were drawn from samurai cinema, with Nate Fox citing Sanjuro's climactic duel. Their tension comes from stillness, wind and "upwards of 60,000 individual leaf particles", some waiting "to be kicked up by a warrior in a duel" ([VFX Voice](https://vfxvoice.com/creating-the-way-of-the-warrior-for-ghost-of-tsushima/)). Sekiro builds the duel around posture and deflects ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2019/03/guide_sekiro_shadows_die_twice_combat_-_posture_deflections_shinobi_prosthetic_and_stealth)), and its fighters barely leave the ground. The mountain temple courtyard should follow this model and stay fully grounded. That makes the later vertical moves read as escalation.

## Many places in seconds: center the action, let the background sell the place

The camera grammar for showing environments fast rests on one ufotable principle. J.B. Siraudin's shot analysis of Demon Slayer S2E9 finds the "action is compressed at the center of the frame so that the eye doesn't have to move too much", planned at storyboard with Mad Max: Fury Road as the model. The analysis counts **5 attacks in 8 seconds** with a prepare (long), strike (short, often one frame), relax (long) rhythm, animation on twos with camera shake on ones, and a directional camera bump on each strike ([Siraudin](https://jbsiraudin.github.io/blog/demon-slayer-visual-grammar)). When the action stays centred, cuts can come fast without losing the viewer, and the background is free to change. Each location registers through what surrounds the centre: a pagoda roofline, gas lamps in puddles, fire behind a silhouette, receding fusuma.

Wides establish each place, but with the fight already in them. Ghost of Tsushima's art director Jason Connell notes that "so many Kurosawa movies open with really wide expansive shots, where a person is like drowning in a sea of green" ([Flicks](https://www.flicks.com.au/features/how-the-hit-ps4-game-ghost-of-tsushima-was-influenced-by-kurosawa-and-samurai-cinema/)). The standoff camera then cuts to the combatants approaching and zooms in with letterboxing ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/ghost-of-tsushima-gameplay-trailer-breakdown/)). That fits the courtyard opening. Later locations can establish themselves in under a second with a foreground-occlusion dolly past a lantern or noren curtain, or a drone-style pull-back from a blade lock. John Wick: Chapter 4's overhead Dragon's Breath one-take, inspired by the game Hong Kong Massacre, turns architecture into a readable map ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/1237396/john-wick-4-director-was-inspired-by-video-game-hong-kong-massacre-for-a-key-action-scene-interview)). Director Chad Stahelski hid its only seam at a flare moment, "before we light the guys on fire" ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/1224205/john-wick-4-director-breaks-down-that-fiery-top-shot-sequence-interview)). A top-down shot onto raked courtyard gravel, or straight down the castle's stacked shaft, does the same job.

Ichirō Itano's camera grammar covers close-ups and travel. In "angle animation" the lens widens as the subject approaches, telephoto at distance and fisheye up close. In "moving-body perspective" a subjective camera uses frame-in and frame-out, and with 3DCG "the camera's slight lag becomes especially noticeable" ([Wikipedia: Ichirō Itano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichir%C5%8D_Itano)). Itano's own line, "the chase was more interesting than the attack", argues for a lateral tracking run down the rainy street over another aerial arc. Combined with Nakamura's warning, the rule is to lag the camera 0.08–0.15 s while tracking, and to lock it to the ground at take-off and landing so the world, not the lens, carries the motion. A low, ground-locked shot with fighters passing overhead proves height better than any camera that follows them up.

Pacing should be felt, not formulaic. Game trailer editor Derek Lieu argues that "MORE editing does not mean BETTER editing" ([Derek Lieu, 2023](https://derek-lieu-yrd2.squarespace.com/blog/2023/4/23/more-trailer-editing-is-not-better-trailer-editing)). He finds that "sometimes two frames off looks more in sync than what's in sync", and uses about 4-frame audio crossfades between clips ([Derek Lieu, 2024](https://derek-lieu-yrd2.squarespace.com/blog/2024/5/5/professional-video-editing-tidbits)). Feature-film action averages about 4 s per shot ([Stephen Follows](https://stephenfollows.com/many-shots-average-movie/)). No reliable trailer-specific cut rate was found, and no published shot counts exist for the reference game trailers; measuring them with PySceneDetect is the recommended next step. The wide-to-close split below (about 30–40% wides, 40–50% mediums and two-shots, 15–25% inserts) is a working estimate, not a measured norm. The intensity map raises cut rate location by location, places the breath before the steepest climb, and ends with a cross-cut montage that flashes the same duel through all four places on consecutive beats.

## Conclusion

The research changes the problem from "make the leaps look heavier" to "stop asking leaps to move the fight". Every documented production, from ufotable's films to Arc System Works and CyberConnect2, moves the duel between places with something that has a cause: a hit, a collapse, a cut, a world that rearranges itself. The visible airtime that remains is a garnish measured in tenths of a second. That is good news for a procedural trailer, because cuts and knock-throughs are far cheaper to make convincing than a two-second aerial arc. The current code also already has most of the ingredients: `hitStop`, `impactFrame('white'|'ink'|'invert')`, `speedLines`, `dust`, `debris`, `clashBurst` and afterimages. What is missing is mostly orchestration and the jump's gravity profile.

The evidence is weaker on exact numbers. No source gives anime frame counts for jump phases, trailer cut rates or wide-to-close ratios, and several famous on-screen devices remain viewing leads rather than documented facts. The values above should be tuned by eye on captured frames from `tools/trailer-capture.mjs`, not treated as fixed. The one firm rule is Murch's: if a choice trades emotion or rhythm for spatial continuity, choose emotion and rhythm, and let the cut do the travelling.

## Prioritized techniques for the Haru and Mitsuki trailer

The order is by impact on the "floating" complaint per unit of work. Hooks name existing functions in `game/trailer.js`, `game/combat.js` and `game/anime-fx.js`.

| Priority | Technique | What it replaces or fixes | Procedural implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Remove every on-screen two-person landmark leap** | The `'leap'` beat in all four `ROUNDS` entries | Reposition between landmarks off-screen. Frame the crouch and push-off, cut, then open the next shot already mid-exchange at the new spot (teleport both roots during the cut). Use the location's walkable ground for the new mark and its viewpoints for the camera. |
| 2 | **One distinct seam device per location change** | Launch into a white-out, then a drop from the sky | Courtyard → street: match on action with the slash direction and screen position kept, audio pre-lap. Street → rooftops: lamp slash, fire flare, `impactFrame('ink')`. Rooftops → castle: knock-through plus a fall. Enter each world mid-clash, never with an arrival. |
| 3 | **Asymmetric, short jump physics for any jump that remains** | `arc = 4·h·u·(1−u)` with eased horizontal travel over 1.15–2.1 s | Derive g_up = 2h/t_up² with t_up 0.25–0.4 s and g_down = 2.5–3.5× g_up. Apex hang of 0.06–0.15 s at 0.3–0.5× gravity only while holding a strike pose. Linear or ease-out horizontal travel. Cap airtime at about 0.7 s, and break anything longer with a clash, a wall kick or a cut. |
| 4 | **Contact events at both ends of every airborne moment** | `play(f,'jump',.06)` with no anticipation and a soft landing | 0.10–0.20 s IK crouch before take-off. Push-off dust, debris and crack decal. Landing compression deeper than the crouch, 2–4 frames of hit-stop, vertical shake, skid with a dust cone, and a decal that stays (Vlambeer permanence). |
| 5 | **Ground-locked camera at take-off and landing; lagged camera while tracking** | Cameras that ride the pair through the sky | Hold or slow the camera on a ground landmark for the take-off and landing beats. While tracking, use a critically damped spring with 0.08–0.15 s lag. Never pan faster than the fighters' contact rhythm. Pick the most intense angle per beat from several candidate viewpoints, as ufotable does. |
| 6 | **Desynchronise the two fighters** | Matching arcs and simultaneous take-off | Offset take-offs by 0.1–0.25 s, vary arc heights and speeds, and make the attacker follow a victim driven by the hit rather than a shared leap. |
| 7 | **Contact shadow and environment-matched rim light** | Shadows that may fade or detach mid-air | A blob or decal shadow under each fighter on the walkable ground, scaling and fading with height but never vanishing. Rim light colour and direction taken from each location's key (sun, gas lamp, fire, castle lanterns), per Terao. |
| 8 | **Ground travel and surface contact as the "movement" vocabulary** | Long arcs as the only way to cover distance | Lateral running exchanges down the street. Rooftop ridge sprints as 3–5 hops of 0.35–0.45 s. Wall kicks via raycast plant and reflected relaunch. Roof slides with a tile-chip emitter. Skid-stops at 30–60 m/s². A 0.3–0.5 m landing snap so feet never hover. |
